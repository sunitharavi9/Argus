### [When Words Are Safe But Actions Kill: Probing Physical Danger Beyond Text Safety in Hidden-State Risk Space](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15218v1)
**Authors:** Weimeng Wang, Ziqiang Wang, Zihang Zhan | **Published:** 2026-07-16

**Core Problem:** This paper addresses the gap between text-level safety and physically grounded danger in language models, where linguistically benign instructions can become unsafe once grounded in the physical world.

**Approach:**
- Utilized hidden-state direction analysis and random-split null tests to study the relationship between content danger and physical danger in language models.
- Proposed PRISM, a single-layer L2-regularized logistic probe over full hidden states, to detect physical danger.
- Tested PRISM on the SafeAgentBench benchmark.

**Results:**
- Demonstrated that content danger and physical danger form separable signals in language model representations.
- Achieved 86.2-87.7% accuracy on SafeAgentBench with 11.7-13.7% false positive rate using PRISM.
- Showed that same-scale language models can over-block safe tasks at a higher false positive rate.

### [MedFailBench: A Clinician-Built Open-Source Benchmark for Medical AI Safety Boundary Inspection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15166v1)
**Authors:** Goktug Ozkan | **Published:** 2026-07-16

**Core Problem:** This paper addresses the need for a benchmark to evaluate medical AI safety by identifying which safety boundary failed, rather than just measuring correct answers.

**Approach:**
- Developed MedFailBench, a clinician-built synthetic benchmark for medical AI safety boundary inspection.
- Created a failure atlas that labels medical AI errors by severity and safety gate type.
- Established a live HuggingFace leaderboard preview and an automated pipeline for archiving model-response screening runs.

**Results:**
- Released the current public version (v0.2.1) of MedFailBench, containing 44 clinician-reviewed synthetic cases with severity annotations.
- Provided a safety gate taxonomy and a clinical severity rubric.
- Enabled the evaluation of medical AI models on safety boundary inspection.

### [Setup Complete, Now You Are Compromised: Weaponizing Setup Instructions Against AI Coding Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15143v1)
**Authors:** Aadesh Bagmar, Pushkar Saraf | **Published:** 2026-07-16

**Core Problem:** This paper addresses the vulnerability of AI coding agents to supply-chain attacks delivered through ordinary project-setup documentation.

**Approach:**
- Edited README files, requirements files, or Makefiles to redirect AI coding agents to untrusted registries, vulnerable versions, or wrong-but-plausible names.
- Probed frontier models on twelve scenarios in five attack classes, grounded in documented incidents.
- Demonstrated the feasibility of package-install-time supply-chain attacks.

**Results:**
- Showed that AI coding agents can be compromised through setup instructions without verifying the names, sources, or known vulnerabilities of dependencies.
- Highlighted the need for install-time security measures to prevent supply-chain attacks.

### [Beyond Success Rate: Cost-Aware Evaluation of Offensive and Defensive Security Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15263v1)
**Authors:** Paul Kassianik, Blaine Nelson, Yaron Singer | **Published:** 2026-07-16

**Core Problem:** This paper addresses the limitation of evaluating security agents solely based on success rates, ignoring the cost of reasoning steps, tool calls, and telemetry queries.

**Approach:**
- Introduced a cost-success lens to evaluate language-model security agents on offensive Cybench challenges and defensive Splunk BOTS v1 investigation challenges.
- Compared models at fixed cost levels and decomposed performance by inference spend and tool spend.

**Results:**
- Demonstrated distinct scaling regimes for red- and blue-team tasks, with offensive CTF performance improving with additional test-time budget.
- Highlighted the importance of considering cost in security agent evaluation.

### [Bridge Evidence: Static Retrieval Utility Does Not Predict Causal Utility in Multi-Step Agentic Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15253v1)
**Authors:** Debayan Mukhopadhyay, Utshab Kumar Ghosh, Shubham Chatterjee | **Published:** 2026-07-16

**Core Problem:** This paper addresses the limitation of evaluating retrieval systems based on static usefulness, which may not predict causal utility in multi-step agentic search.

**Approach:**
- Used a ReAct style agent over HotpotQA to replay 1000 development questions and measure the gap between static and causal utility.
- Compared the original run against its counterfactual, deleting a document and re-running the rest of the trajectory from that point.

**Results:**
- Demonstrated that static retrieval utility does not predict causal utility in multi-step agentic search.
- Highlighted the need for a more nuanced evaluation of retrieval systems.

### [Pretraining Data Can Be Poisoned through Computational Propaganda](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15267v1)
**Authors:** Victoria Graf, Hannaneh Hajishirzi, Noah A. Smith | **Published:** 2026-07-16

**Core Problem:** This paper addresses the vulnerability of pretraining data to poisoning attacks through computational propaganda.

**Approach:**

- Introduced HalfLife, a novel analysis for estimating adversarial content inclusion in web-crawl based language model training data.

**Results:**
- Showed that poisoning attacks on pretraining data can introduce harmful behaviors to language models.
- Highlighted the need for robust data curation pipelines to mitigate poisoning attacks.
