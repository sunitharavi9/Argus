- The audit demonstrates the causal importance of attributed rows.

### [Weak-to-Strong Generalization via Direct On-Policy Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05394v1)
**Authors:** Shiyuan Feng, Huan-ang Gao, Haohan Chi | **Published:** 2026-07-06
**Core Problem:** Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) is expensive to repeat on every new strong model, and there's a need for a weak-to-strong alternative.
**Approach:**
- Proposes Direct On-Policy Distillation (Direct-OPD), which transfers the teacher's RL-induced policy shift instead of directly distilling the post-RL weak teacher.
- Compares the post-RL teacher with its own pre-RL policy to extract the RL gains.
- Enables the improvement of a stronger target model using the distilled policy shift.
**Results:**
- Direct-OPD provides a weak-to-strong generalization approach for reinforcement learning.
- The method enables the efficient improvement of stronger models using the knowledge gained from weaker models.
- It has the potential to reduce the computational cost and improve the scalability of RLVR.

### [What Does a Discrete Diffusion Model Learn?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05381v1)
**Authors:** Rodrigo Casado Noguerales, Bernhard Schölkopf, Thomas Hofmann | **Published:** 2026-07-06
**Core Problem:** The question of what a discrete diffusion model learns remains unclear, with a need for a rigorous understanding of the learned process.
**Approach:**
- Derives the continuous-time Markov chain (CTMC) ELBO for any noising process, including boundary terms.
- Proves the Oracle Distance theorem, which states that the negative ELBO is exactly equal to the data entropy plus the path KL from the oracle reverse process to the learned one.
- Demonstrates that the unique optimizer of the ELBO is the conditional expectation of the true reverse jump rate given the current noisy state.
**Results:**
- Provides a rigorous understanding of what a discrete diffusion model learns, focusing on the learned denoiser, score ratio, or bridge plug-in predictor.
- The Oracle Distance theorem offers a novel insight into the optimization process of discrete diffusion models.
- The approach has the potential to advance the development of more efficient and effective diffusion models.
