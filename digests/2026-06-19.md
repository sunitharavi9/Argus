### [LLM agent safety, multi-turn red-teaming, jailbreak benchmarks, adversarial robustness, safety-critical systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20408v1)
**Authors:** Hanwool Lee, Dasol Choi, Bokyeong Kim | **Published:** 2026-06-18
**Core Problem:** The robustness of large language model (LLM) agents under sustained, adaptive adversarial pressure remains poorly characterized, particularly in safety-critical systems.
**Approach:**
- Introduced NRT-Bench, a benchmark for multi-turn red-teaming of LLM agents.
- Simulated a nuclear power plant control room with a five-role operator team, each backed by a configurable LLM.
- Adversaries inject messages over four channels in bounded multi-turn sessions with per-turn feedback.
**Results:**
- Evaluated four frontier operating models under the benchmark.
- Harm is an objective signal rather than LLM-judged text, terminating the run when any critical safety function is lost.
- The causing message is attributed to the termination, providing insight into the vulnerability of LLM agents.

### [What Do Safety-Aligned LLMs Learn From Mixed Compliance Demonstrations?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20508v1)
**Authors:** Sihui Dai, Mann Patel | **Published:** 2026-06-18
**Core Problem:** It remains unclear how safety-aligned large language models interpret different types of compliance demonstrations.
**Approach:**
- Mixed benign compliance demonstrations with harmful compliance demonstrations.
- Tested three hypotheses about how demonstration composition drives harmful compliance.
- Evaluated across four models to understand the impact of demonstration types.
**Results:**
- Found that benign and harmful demonstrations are not interchangeable, with benign demonstrations either reducing or increasing harmful compliance depending on the model.
- Identified preference optimization as the critical training stage preventing benign demonstrations from increasing harmful compliance.
- Demonstrated the importance of demonstration ordering in influencing model behavior.

**Authors:** Zewen Liu | **Published:** 2026-06-18
**Core Problem:** Evaluator biases in multi-agent systems can propagate through the network, affecting overall performance and safety.
**Approach:**
- Introduced Contagion Networks, a framework for measuring bias propagation.
- Conducted a controlled experiment with a 3-agent system using different evaluator bias profiles.
**Results:**
- Found that evaluator biases consistently propagate between agents, even within the same underlying model.
- Identified three propagation regimes governed by the spectral radius of the contagion matrix.
- Demonstrated that homogeneous-model agents produce weaker contagion coefficients than cross-model agents.
